From an idea to the pattern

I crochet, so I know the feeling: the idea is easy, and the pattern is the hard part. I first explored that problem in a class project, then built a tool of my own.

Step one · Class collaboration on ReplitFall 2026

Notch

On a class team, I helped build Notch on Replit: a design companion that drafts skirt and top patterns from body measurements and prints them at actual size. I also delivered our pitch and researched the market, which showed 3,186 U.S. fashion design degrees awarded in 2024 against about 2,500 job openings a year.

Step two · Independent project, built with Claude CodeFall 2026

My personal pattern generator

Notch was designed for fashion students, so I built a separate tool for crochet with Claude Code. It turns photos, sketches and notes into a crochet pattern, and code checks every row’s stitch counts before the pattern is displayed.

Idea board

Pin references, tag what to use from each, and refine the AI’s design brief.

Multi-piece planning

Large projects are planned and drafted piece by piece, so an error in one piece doesn’t affect the others.

Stitch checker

Code verifies each row’s stitch count and shaping; errors go back to the AI for one targeted repair.

Progress tracking

Check off rows while working and add notes that inform the next revision.

Originality check

New patterns are compared row by row against a library of licensed reference patterns.

Featured project · Environmental advocacy through ‘artivism’

Still life as climate advocacy: YoungArts Collection

1.3%Named a 2026 YoungArts Winner with Distinction in Visual Arts, one of 171 artists selected from nearly 13,000 applicants nationwide.

My YoungArts submission was a series of still lifes that uses fruit, one of the oldest subjects in art, to question the agricultural system behind it. Each piece deals with a hidden cost of how our food is grown, from the overuse of synthetic pesticides and fertilizers to monocropping that strips soil of its health and pushes once-fertile land toward desertification. By distorting familiar forms, I wanted viewers to look twice at something they usually take for granted. The award included a week-long intensive in Miami and a scholarship.

About my program

USC Iovine and Young Academy (IYA) was founded in 2013 with a $70 million gift from music and tech entrepreneurs Jimmy Iovine and Andre “Dr. Dre” Young. It teaches design, technology and business together through hands-on challenges, often set by real companies. It is highly selective: USC reported an 8% acceptance rate in 2018, the year its first class of 30 graduated. In January 2026 it announced a third bachelor’s degree, Human Technology Interaction, which USC describes as the first undergraduate program of its kind in the country; its first students arrive in fall 2026.

Business of Innovation is a new joint B.S. from IYA and the Marshall School of Business, launched in fall 2025. My class is one of its first. It pairs Marshall’s business core with IYA’s human-centered design and technology work, and ends with a senior “Garage” experience where teams take a product from concept to launch.
